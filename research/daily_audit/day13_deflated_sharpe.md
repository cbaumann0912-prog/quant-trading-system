# Day 13 Research Audit — Deflated Sharpe Ratio: Current Walk-Forward Output

> Supersedes the original version of this audit, which applied the DSR to the archived FVG_BoS_Reversal strategy. That analysis rested on 66–79 non-zero daily observations spread across 15 calendar years, and its script had been unexecutable since `PerformanceAnalyzer.__init__` dropped its `ann_factor` argument — meaning the published figures could not be regenerated by the code that claimed to produce them. Prior version retained in git history.

## What question was investigated?

Do any of the six signal configurations currently in `results/` survive deflation once selection bias and non-normality are accounted for?

The Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey & López de Prado, 2014) returns the probability that an observed Sharpe is a true positive, given the number of configurations searched and the higher moments of the return distribution.

## Why does the question matter?

`run_research.py` reports a raw per-window Sharpe. Six configurations were evaluated. Reporting the best of six as though it were a single pre-registered test overstates evidence, and the amount of overstatement is exactly what the DSR quantifies. Any Sharpe that reaches the paper needs this adjustment attached or it is not a defensible claim.

## What methodology was used?

For each report in `results/`, the out-of-sample bar-level P&L series is rebuilt by replaying the pipeline that produced it. Parameters are read back out of the JSON rather than re-specified, so the reconstruction cannot drift from the published run.

Per-window P&L is `exposure.shift(1) * log_returns` restricted to the test slice, identical to `run_research.window_sharpe`. The shift is applied after slicing, so the first bar of each test window is dropped rather than inheriting an exposure formed at the embargo boundary. Windows are concatenated into one pooled out-of-sample series per configuration — 771 observations, against 66–79 in the superseded audit.

`n_trials = 6` is the documented grid: 3 pairs × 2 signals. A sweep over N ∈ {1, 6, 25, 100, 500} is reported alongside it.

Script: `research/applied_analysis/day13_deflated_sharpe.py`

## What assumptions were made?

**Annualization.** `deflated_sharpe_ratio` de-annualizes its input via `compute_ann_factor()`, which divides observation count by the calendar span of the returns index. The pooled series is discontinuous — test windows are separated by training and embargo regions — so its raw span understates bars per year and would inflate the de-annualized Sharpe. The analyzer is handed a synthetic contiguous index calibrated to the sample-level factor recorded in the report (312.46 bars/year). The round trip is asserted in code, not assumed.

**Kurtosis convention.** The `V` term uses `(kurtosis + 2) / 4`, which is correct for *excess* kurtosis and reduces to 0.5 under normality. `scipy.stats.kurtosis` returns excess by default. Verified consistent.

**Trial count.** `n_trials = 6` counts the configurations in `results/` and nothing else. It is a *within-grid* figure, not the project's cumulative trial count.

The project does maintain a cumulative record — the strategy roster in `README.md` and the per-strategy files in `research/strategies/validation_falsification/`, under a stated policy that trial count is cumulative and includes failed tests. Six strategies have been closed, several carrying multiple independent tests (Strategy 1 alone ran three; Strategy 3 ran tests 2, 2b and 2c).

The momentum and mean-reversion diagnostics scored here are *additional* to those, not the same six. The correct `n_trials` for any deflation quoted in the paper must be sourced from the cumulative roster, and it is materially larger than 6. The N = 25 / 100 / 500 columns are included to bracket that range. **Every figure in the N = 6 column is therefore optimistic.**

**Costs.** All figures are gross of transaction costs and unsized, consistent with the underlying reports.

## What were the findings?

| Config | n_obs | Sharpe | Skew | Ex. Kurt | N=1 | N=6 | N=25 | N=100 | N=500 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EURUSD mean-reversion | 771 | −0.386 | −0.679 | 28.50 | 0.2709 | 0.0281 | 0.0046 | 0.0008 | 0.0001 |
| EURUSD momentum | 771 | 0.539 | 0.021 | 1.17 | 0.8012 | 0.3249 | 0.1249 | 0.0460 | 0.0137 |
| GBPUSD mean-reversion | 771 | −0.720 | −9.634 | 177.49 | 0.0857 | 0.0038 | 0.0004 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |
| GBPUSD momentum | 771 | 0.201 | 1.158 | 15.35 | 0.6246 | 0.1629 | 0.0465 | 0.0134 | 0.0031 |
| USDJPY mean-reversion | 771 | −0.261 | −0.609 | 53.85 | 0.3409 | 0.0436 | 0.0080 | 0.0016 | 0.0003 |
| USDJPY momentum | 771 | 0.899 | −0.267 | 4.65 | 0.9190 | 0.5390 | 0.2746 | 0.1287 | 0.0490 |

**No configuration reaches DSR ≥ 0.95 at any trial count, including N = 1.**

That last point is the one that matters. At N = 1 the DSR applies no selection penalty at all — it is asking only whether a single pre-registered strategy clears zero. The best configuration, USDJPY momentum, reaches 0.919. Multiple testing is not what sinks these results; 771 observations at Sharpe 0.9 simply is not enough evidence.

Mean-reversion is negative on all three pairs, consistent with the negative mean IC already reported (`ic_summary.mean` = −0.368 for EURUSD momentum, with the expected-sign check failing).

**Pooled Sharpe is not the mean of per-window Sharpes:**

| Config | pooled SR | mean window SR |
|---|---|---|
| EURUSD mean-reversion | −0.386 | **+0.057** |
| EURUSD momentum | 0.539 | 0.421 |
| GBPUSD mean-reversion | −0.720 | −0.258 |
| GBPUSD momentum | 0.201 | 0.016 |
| USDJPY mean-reversion | −0.261 | −0.212 |
| USDJPY momentum | 0.899 | 0.988 |

EURUSD mean-reversion **changes sign** between the two. Averaging per-window Sharpes weights each window equally regardless of how much of the variance it contains, and a Sharpe is a ratio — its mean across subsamples is not the ratio of the pooled moments. The pooled figure is the one to quote.

## What are alternative explanations?

1. **Signal degeneracy, not absent edge.** `ic_status_counts` shows `constant_signal` on 4 of 10 windows for EURUSD momentum — the sign-valued signal never changed within those test slices, so IC was undefined. A signal that is flat 40% of the time is being measured partly on its inactivity.

2. **GBPUSD mean-reversion is not a distributional result.** Skew −9.6 with excess kurtosis 177 means a handful of bars dominate the series. Its Sharpe describes a few events, not a process. The DSR's Student-t non-normality correction is stretched well past where it is reliable.

3. **771 bars is thin.** At three test months per window over ten windows, the pooled sample covers roughly 2.5 years of out-of-sample time. Regime coverage is limited.

4. **Costs would worsen every figure.** These are gross. A momentum signal flipping at the bar level accrues real spread cost, and USDJPY momentum's 0.899 is the number most exposed to it.

## What are next steps?

- Reconcile this grid with the cumulative roster. The roster is maintained manually in `README.md`; these six diagnostics are not in it. A machine-readable trial log written by `run_research.py` at run time would let `n_trials` be read rather than assembled by hand, and would close the gap between the two records.
- Diagnose the `constant_signal` windows before interpreting momentum results further.
- Add transaction costs before any Sharpe is quoted outside this repo.
- Re-run after cost modelling and compare. If USDJPY momentum survives costs and still fails deflation, the configuration should be dropped rather than tuned — tuning it would inflate the trial count that already sinks it.
- The lockbox (≥ 2024-01-01) remains untouched and must stay that way until a single final evaluation.
