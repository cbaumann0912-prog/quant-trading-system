# Day 32 — Markowitz on Forex: Weights Table & Sensitivity

## Methodology
Selected 10 evenly-spaced points from the 50-point target-return sweep (range: global min-variance return up to 1.5x the highest single-pair mean return), extracting markowitz_weights() output at each. Computed weight sensitivity (Δweight/Δtarget_return) between every consecutive pair of the 10 points, and flagged any sign flips across the range. Data: 2011-01-03 to 2023-12-29, daily log returns, n=4054 aligned observations. Nineteen of the fifty swept targets sat above the achievable range on this sample and were skipped as infeasible.

## Findings
- Weights table (10 points, target_return 0.000033 to 0.000137): EUR/USD declines from 0.404 to 0.170 across the first nine points (monotonic, stays positive throughout). GBP/USD declines from 0.156 to -0.121, crossing zero between target_return 0.000078 (weight +0.023) and 0.000092 (weight -0.018). USD/JPY rises from 0.440 to 0.951, staying positive throughout and approaching but not exceeding 100% allocation. The tenth and final point breaks the pattern entirely: EUR/USD 0.593, GBP/USD -0.593, USD/JPY exactly 1.000.
- Sensitivity: identical slope across the first 8 of 9 consecutive intervals — EUR/USD -2496.42, GBP/USD -2955.95, USD/JPY +5452.37 (Δweight per unit Δtarget_return). The ninth interval departs sharply: EUR/USD +40557.99, GBP/USD -45289.35, USD/JPY +4731.36. USD/JPY landing on exactly 1.000000 at that point identifies it as a boundary solution rather than an interior one, which is also where the adjacent infeasible targets begin.
- The constant slope over the interior region is a structural consequence of the closed-form solution: x = (1/2)Σ⁻¹(λp̄ - νones) is linear in target_return for fixed Σ and p̄, since λ and ν themselves solve a linear 2x2 system with target_return only on the right-hand side. The GBP/USD sign flip is therefore not a threshold effect or instability onset — it is the deterministic point where an already-fixed straight line crosses zero. The break at the final point is the opposite case and worth separating: linearity is a property of the unconstrained interior solution, and it stops describing the optimizer once a constraint binds.
- Only GBP/USD flips sign in this range; EUR/USD and USD/JPY remain positive throughout.

## Interpretation
The constant weight sensitivity over the interior region is mathematically expected and makes the optimizer's behavior predictable there, but it does not make the resulting allocations more trustworthy, and the predictability itself is bounded — it holds only while no constraint binds. Rather than indicating instability, the linear relationship shows that increasingly aggressive short and leveraged positions are a deterministic consequence of the unconstrained Markowitz solution when target return is increased. The real concern is therefore not unpredictable optimization behavior, but the optimizer's systematic amplification of noisy expected-return estimates into economically extreme portfolios, reinforcing the need for out-of-sample validation, shrinkage, and practical portfolio constraints.
